The goal of this short note is to provide a heuristic on why the limit(s)
of finite-number-of-particles log gases in the disk are empty for $\beta=4$.

Recall that we consider the Gibbs point process
\[
\P_n(d\gamma)
=
\frac{1}{Z_{N,\beta}}
e^{-\beta H(\gamma)}
\Bin_{\D,n}(d\gamma),
\]
where $\Bin_{\D,n}$ is the binomial point process in the unit disk $\D$ and
\[
H(\gamma)
=
-\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\gamma}\log |x-y|.
\]

For a class $\mathcal{C}$ of configurations such that, for all
$\gamma\in\mathcal{C}$,
\[
H(\gamma)\simeq \mathrm{Cst},
\]
the restricted free energy is given by
\[
F_{n,\beta}(\mathcal{C})
:=
-\frac{1}{\beta}
\log\!\int_{\mathcal{C}}
e^{-\beta H(\gamma)}
\,\Bin_{\D,n}(d\gamma)
\simeq
\mathrm{Cst}
-\frac{1}{\beta}
\log \Bin_{\D,n}(\mathcal{C}).
\]

This quantity allows us to compare how probable different classes of
configurations are. We know (at least for $\beta=2$), that almost all particles concentrate 
 on the boundary of the disk. To see where the $\beta=4$ comes from we will compare the asymptotics
of candidates to be typical configuration:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The first candidate will be configurations where all the particles goes to the boundary leaving a limiting empty interior
\item The second candidate will be configurations with essentially all points on the boundary but one remains near $0$
\end{enumerate}
We will see that for $\beta>4$ the free energy cost of candidates $1$ will be favorable. This suggest empty point processes at the limit.


Let us define our two types of configurations:
$\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$.

Let $n\geq 2$ and denote by
\[
\Gamma_n
:=
\{\gamma\subset\D:\#\gamma=n\}
\]
the space of configurations with $n$ points in the unit disk.

 For $m\geq1$, write
\[
\zeta_k^{(m)}
:=
e^{2\pi i k/m},
k=0,\ldots,m-1.
\]



We define the reference class $\mathcal{C}_1$ by
\[
\mathcal{C}_1
:=
\left\{
\gamma\in\Gamma_n:
\#\bigl(\gamma\cap B(\zeta_k^{(m)},1/n)\cap\D\bigr)=1
\text{ for } k=0,\ldots,n-1
\right\}.
\]

Let $\alpha>0$ be small.
Similarly, we define the reference class $\mathcal{C}_2$ by
\[
\mathcal{C}_2
:=
\left\{
\gamma\in\Gamma_n:
\begin{array}{l}
\#\bigl(\gamma\cap B(0,\sqrt{\alpha/\pi})\bigr)=1,\\[2pt]
\#\bigl(\gamma\cap B(\zeta_k^{(m)},1/n)\bigr)=1
\text{ for } k=0,\ldots,n-2
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

Thus there is exactly one point in the central ball and one point in each of
the $n-1$ blue boundary regions, with no points elsewhere.



We now compute the binomial probabilities of these two classes. Write $|E|$
for planar area and set
\[
q_n
:=
\frac{|B(1,1/n)\cap\mathbb{D}|}{\pi}.
\]

Thus $q_n$ is the contribution of one boundary cap.
 The central ball contributes $\alpha$.


We then have
\[
\Bin_{\mathbb{D},n}\bigl(\mathcal{C}_1\bigr)
=
n!\,q_n^n,
\qquad
\Bin_{\mathbb{D},n}\bigl(\mathcal{C}_2\bigr)
=
n!\,\alpha^2 q_n^{n-1}.
\]



We have
\[
q_n
\simeq
\frac{1}{2n^2}.
\]

Hence
\[
\Bin_{\mathbb{D},n}(\mathcal{C}_1)
\simeq
n!\,2^{-n}n^{2n},
\qquad
\Bin_{\mathbb{D},n}(\mathcal{C}_2)
\simeq
n!\alpha^2\,2^{1-n}n^{2-2n}.
\]





Set
$$
\gamma_1^{(n)}:=\{\zeta_0^{(n)},\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}^{(n)}\},\
\qquad\
\gamma_2^{(n)}:=\{0,\zeta_0^{(n-1)},\ldots,\zeta_{n-2}^{(n-1)}\}.\
$$
the prototypical configurations associated respectively to $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$.

Because of the repulsion, we expect that the points near the boundary are distributed 
close $\gamma_1^{(n)}$ and $\gamma_2^{(n)}$, so we trust the approximations
$$
H(\gamma)\simeq H(\gamma_i^{(n)}),\quad \forall \gamma\in \mathcal{C}_i, \quad i\in\{0,1\}.
$$
The classical computation gives


\[
H(\gamma_1^{(n)})
=
-\frac{n}{2}\log n
\]
and
\[
H(\gamma_2^{(n)})
=-\frac{n-1}{2}\log(n-1).
\]
where the impact of the central point just dispear since the zeta's are the 
roots of unity.
In particular,
\[
H(\gamma_1^{(n)})-H(\gamma_2^{(n)})
=
\frac{1}{2}\log n+\frac{1}{2}+O(n^{-1}),
\qquad n\to\infty.
\]
Thus the free energy discrepency is

\[
\Delta_n:=F_{n,\beta}(\mathcal{C}_1)-F_{n,\beta}(\mathcal{C}_2)=\frac{1}{2}\log n-\frac{2}{\beta}\log n+\frac{1}{2}+O(1).
\]

Hence, for $\beta<4$, $\Delta_n \to -\infty$ and for $\beta>4$, $\Delta_n \to \infty$. Hence,
in the first case $\mathcal{C}_2$ is more favorable while in the second case it is  
$\mathcal{C}_1$ that is favored.
